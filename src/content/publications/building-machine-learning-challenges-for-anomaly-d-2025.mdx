Scientific discoveries are often made by finding a pattern or object that was not predicted by
the known rules of science. Oftentimes, these anomalous events or objects that do not conform to
the norms are an indication that the rules of science governing the data are incomplete, and
something new needs to be present to explain these unexpected outliers.

The challenge of finding anomalies can be confounding since it requires codifying a complete
knowledge of the known scientific behaviors and then projecting these known behaviors on the data
to look for deviations. When utilizing machine learning, this presents a particular challenge since
we require that the model not only understands scientific data perfectly but also recognizes when
the data is inconsistent and out of the scope of its trained behavior.

In this paper, we present three datasets aimed at developing machine learning-based anomaly
detection for disparate scientific domains covering astrophysics, genomics, and polar science. We
present the different datasets along with a scheme to make machine learning challenges around the
three datasets findable, accessible, interoperable, and reusable (FAIR).

Furthermore, we present an approach that generalizes to future machine learning challenges,
enabling the possibility of large, more compute-intensive challenges that can ultimately lead to
scientific discovery.
